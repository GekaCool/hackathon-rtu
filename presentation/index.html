Let's be honest: when did you last visit a museum because you really wanted to? Most exhibitions are still a row of objects behind glass with a text label. 27 seconds — the average time a visitor looks at a work of art. Museum fatigue sets in after 20 to 30 minutes. For children, a museum often feels like a duty rather than an adventure. Museums invest in gamification, immersive rooms and lighting. But one sense is almost completely ignored: sound.

Exhibit Voice is a real-time audio companion for museums. Every exhibit gets its own sound story, and it changes depending on how close you are. From far away you hear ambient sound — distant sword clashes, a bird singing, an engine starting. As you come closer, the sound grows louder and draws you toward the exhibit. Right next to it, the story begins: Karl Benz tells you how he built the first car; an endangered bird sings, then explains why it is disappearing. Visitors follow a sound instead of reading a label.

The technology is simple and affordable. Each exhibit gets a small Bluetooth beacon. The visitor's phone measures signal strength, and volume follows distance. No app to download: scan one QR code at the entrance and a web app opens in the browser. All you need is a phone and headphones. To our knowledge this isn't offered in Latvian museums yet — and it needs no rebuilding of exhibition halls.

Sound gets people interested. But what if they want to know more? Each exhibit is connected to an AI assistant built on the museum's own curated texts, using RAG. A visitor can ask "Who wore this helmet?" or "How fast could this car go?" and get an answer from verified museum content — not random internet facts.

Children come back: in a Madeira museum study, 84.6% of children with audio guides wanted to return, vs 33.3% without. People learn more: a beacon-based museum game in the MUSEON study raised learning scores from 56.4% to 74.6%. Naturkunde Museum Berlin already uses bird sounds that grow louder as you approach. Our difference: most guides just play a clip on arrival — we create an evolving soundscape that pulls visitors across the room.
